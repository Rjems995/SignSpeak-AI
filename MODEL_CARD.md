# SignSpeak ASL alphabet models

**Coverage:** all 26 letters. A-I and K-Y use a static network. J/Z require a short recorded movement sequence. Models support experimental, human-reviewed fingerspelling and informal practice, not full language translation or proficiency assessment.

**Static architecture:** StandardScaler -> dense 96 ReLU -> dense 64 ReLU -> dense 24 softmax. Input is 21 image-space XY landmarks (42 values), wrist-centered, palm-length scaled, and mirrored by palm geometry. Image-plane direction is deliberately preserved: arbitrary rotation invariance erases distinctions such as K/P, G/Q, and H/U. Feature version: `wrist-scale-orientation-xy-v2`.

**Motion architecture:** StandardScaler -> dense 48 ReLU -> dense 24 ReLU -> three-class softmax (J, Z, unsupported). Real timestamped sequences are trimmed to their active interval and resampled at 16 time steps for five joints: wrist, thumb tip, index tip, middle tip, little tip. Coordinates remain relative to the initial wrist, with median palm scaling and mirror handling. At least eight valid frames spanning 250 ms are required; stationary, non-finite and severely interrupted sequences are rejected. Feature math/version remains `temporal-palm-scale-16x5xy-v1`. The browser targets approximately 30 fps while recording, allows 4.5 seconds, and supports early finish.

**Static source/splits:** the pinned SigNN Apache-2.0 coordinate file in `datasets/`. Canonical deduplication precedes per-class sample splitting: 622 unique samples, 373 train, 124 validation, 125 test. Q has just two examples (one training, one test), no validation examples, and a fixed conservative gate. Other static gates use validation-only distances. Training augmentation happens after splitting.

**Motion source/splits:** 188 accepted J/Z recordings plus 10 real non-J/Z controls from SigNN V1 (CC0) and SignAlphaSet dynamic V3 (CC BY 4.0). Source clips split into 119 train, 39 validation and 40 test. The original 46 clips retain their frozen split and extraction. No identical source-video hashes or canonical trajectory fingerprints cross partitions. Per-clip participant IDs are unavailable, so these are not signer-independent splits. Gates use validation clips; references and support points use training clips. See `datasets/motion-expanded-provenance.json`, `motion-sources.json`, `motion-expansion-audit.json`, and [MOTION_DATA.md](MOTION_DATA.md).

**Measured performance:** static raw accuracy 74.4%, gated accuracy 68.8% over 125 held-out samples. Motion raw J/Z accuracy 97.37%, gated J/Z accuracy 84.21% over 38 held-out positive clips; two additional non-J/Z controls are both rejected. On the same 38 positives, the old model scores 100% raw and 71.05% gated. The new model trades one additional raw Z miss (predicted unsupported) for more correct accepted movements. On the original ten test clips, gated accuracy rises from 70% to 90%. Gated accuracy counts rejected valid signs as mistakes. Q's single static held-out sample was not correctly classified.

**Independent motion research benchmark:** five annotated J/Z performances from three source-identified native ASL signers plus eleven D/I/L controls, from ASLLVD. Source terms restrict redistribution and commercial use; it contributes no training, validation, support or references, and raw clips/coordinates stay ignored. Only aggregate results are bundled. Correct accepted J/Z predictions improve from 0/5 to 3/5; raw classification drops from 5/5 to 4/5; both models reject all eleven controls. This tiny benchmark does not establish broad unseen-signer accuracy. Extraction uses the frontal panel, corrects 15-fps playback to the 60-fps capture clock, and follows source sign boundaries. Full details and terms are in MOTION_DATA.md.

**Rejection:** confidence threshold (default .85), nearest training-support distance, one-hand requirement, static temporal consensus/hold/release, an explicit movement gate and a learned non-J/Z output. Motion false acceptance is 0/2 on held-out training-source controls and 0/11 on independent research controls; these sets are too small for a robust unknown-motion estimate. Static unsupported-pose false acceptance remains unmeasured. Both classifiers can still confidently accept unfamiliar shapes or motions. Confidence is not calibrated.

**Limits:** small uneven classes; sparse Q and similar closed-hand shapes in the unchanged static model; missing public motion signer/session/condition IDs; possible correlated recording/person leakage despite exact duplicate checks; older static versus current live landmark-extractor differences; no depth features. Detector-derived duration, extent and travel-speed proxies are not annotated capture conditions. Slow and large trajectories remain weak, and no broad independent tests establish behavior across handedness, lighting, mobility differences or camera devices. No real live-webcam benchmark was collected. Mirror handling is preprocessing, not an accessibility/fairness evaluation.

**Privacy:** camera frames stay on the device. Live J/Z coordinates are temporarily buffered during explicit recording. Dataset capture requires separate consent; downloads contain coordinates and pseudonymous participant IDs, not footage.

**Reproduce:** see README.md and MOTION_DATA.md. Python and TF.js parity tests cover both feature pipelines and every held-out probability, including the three-output motion model and gates. Split-isolation tests use fingerprints, source-video hashes and frozen clip IDs. The original motion artifacts are retained under `datasets/baselines/motion-v1/` for comparison/rollback. Further evaluation needs consented, participant-disjoint data, balanced letters and diverse unsupported movements reviewed with ASL signers and Deaf educators.
